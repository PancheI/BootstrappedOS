# Your first launch video in an afternoon

A launch video is not a feature tour. It is a 30-second story: one person's problem, the moment it changed, proof, and one thing to do next. The founder approves the story, the silent cut and the final film. The agent writes the beat sheet, captures and cuts.

The rules come from the author's own launch films. Most of his wasted work came from three habits: building before the story was locked, re-recording the voice after the picture changed, and polishing single beats. Module rules (length, references, brand, paid generation): [RULES.md](../../RULES.md).

**References:** none. **The afternoon:** story 30 min, beat sheet 20 min, capture 60 min, silent cut 60 min, voice and captions 30 min, review and export 30 min.

## The process
1. **Story, before you open any tool.** Four lines in plain words:
   1. **Problem:** the moment your customer feels the pain, in *their* words: a real complaint you've heard ("I launched and nobody paid"), not your category name.
   2. **What changed:** the one thing your product does about it. One sentence, no list.
   3. **Proof:** the real product doing it, plus one true number with a visible cause. No number yet? Show before and after.
   4. **One ask:** one action ("Try it at yoursite.com"). Never two.

   **Gate:** the founder approves the four lines.
2. **Beat sheet (30 s default).**

   | Seconds | On screen | Voice |
   |---|---|---|
   | 0-1 | Hook: the problem moment, already moving (no logo, no title card) | The customer's words |
   | 1-8 | The pain, shown: an empty dashboard, an angry message | One short line |
   | 8-12 | The turn: your product appears | "So I built..." |
   | 12-24 | Proof: the real screen doing the one thing, zoomed on what the voice names | What it does |
   | 24-27 | The result: one big number or the after state | The outcome |
   | 27-30 | Logo, one ask, URL | The ask, warm, not hyped |

   For 60 s (a walkthrough), double the proof, not the intro.
3. **Capture real things.**
   - **Screens:** record your real product with the built-in recorder (QuickTime, Windows Game Bar, phone screen recording) in a demo account with believable data. Hide other brands' logos, test card numbers, personal emails and the wrong currency. Never fake a mock-up of your own product: a hand-built checkout got "the checkout is fake, style is not uniform".
   - **Logo:** the real file from `knowledge/brand/brief.md`. Never redraw it.
   - **People:** your own face or voice. A stock-footage montage of strangers was rejected outright, and so was a photoreal 3D version ("we went too far into graphics building").
   - **The moment must be real too:** a launch shown as private messages was rejected; it had to be a public post, because that is what founders actually do.
4. **Silent cut first.** Cut the picture to the beat sheet with no voice, reading the script aloud for timing.
   - **One thing per frame:** one hero (a screen area, a number, a face) and at most 4-6 words. Hard to read? Remove things; don't slow down: "it doesn't have to be slower... just not while showing 5 different dashboards".
   - **Zoom onto what the voice names:** it fills half the width, near the centre. No tiny corner text, no near-empty frames. It must be easy to watch at a glance from the first second.
   - **Keep it moving, calmly:** a change every second or so, but about 12 hard cuts a minute at most and no shot under about 0.7 s.
   - **Show, don't type:** "I see only text; that's not the purpose of a motion designer." Text is for numbers, the product name and captions.
   - **One look:** one font, one accent colour, one background, including the ending. Mixing references beat by beat got "glued, not a coherent motion design".

   **Gate:** the founder approves the silent cut before a word is recorded.
5. **Voice, captions, music.**
   - **Voice:** yours, on your phone, in a quiet room with soft furnishings, is the most trusted. An AI voice is fine; keep one voice for the whole film. End warm, not hyped: an excited closing voice was "annoying".
   - **Lock the script, record one take,** then re-record only the lines that change. Full re-takes for small changes cost many times more than single-line patches.
   - **Muted first:** feeds autoplay silent. Burn in captions (auto-captions, then fix every word by hand), short and big, inside the safe area, never over the proof.
   - **Music:** one licensed track that matches the energy, kept under the voice. A slow track made the product feel slow; a faster one won.
6. **Two formats.**
   - **9:16** (Reels, Shorts, TikTok, LinkedIn mobile): reframe, don't just crop. Bigger zooms, captions in the middle third, nothing key in the top or bottom 15 %.
   - **16:9** (website, YouTube, X): the master.
   - Poster frame: the most emotional moment, not the logo.
7. **Review, then hand over.** Run the quality bar. **Gate:** the founder posts it. Write the post with the core `content-posts` skill.

**Tools (options, no lock-in):** CapCut, Descript, DaVinci Resolve (free), iMovie, Premiere, or Remotion for code. Voice from your phone or any AI voice tool. No paid AI video generator needed.

## Decision rules
- **Sell the feeling, not the feature:** "no one connects to a box". A list of features read over the screens was rejected. What replaced it was founders' real excuses: "I don't have time", "I don't know where to start".
- **Every number needs a visible cause.** "300 signups" appearing from nowhere was cut; sign-ups arriving "via your launch post" stayed.
- **The founder owns the claims.** Flag a risky claim once, in one line; never soften or harden it yourself.
- **Picture changes after the voice mean re-recording,** so the silent cut comes first.
- **Finish the whole film rough, then fix the weakest part.** Don't polish one beat for hours.
- **Want studio-grade motion** (measured from reference films, built in Remotion, generated voice and music)? Use `video-promo-video-playbook`. A vertical reel of this film: `video-short-form-reel`.

## Quality bar
Watch start to end on your phone, muted, then with sound.
- [ ] About 30 seconds (60 only for a walkthrough).
- [ ] Second one already shows the problem, moving.
- [ ] Muted, the story makes sense from captions and pictures alone.
- [ ] Every frame has one focal point; never several dashboards.
- [ ] Every number has a visible cause and is true today.
- [ ] Every product shot is real; no other brands, test data or private details in crops.
- [ ] Voice and picture never contradict (names, "he/she", numbers).
- [ ] Nothing freezes for more than about a second, except the end card.
- [ ] One look, one voice, one ask.
- [ ] Someone who didn't make it watched the whole film once and tried to find what's wrong. Judging from a few stills misses frozen stretches and a "she" said over a man's face. Fix, watch again.

## Never
- Record the voice before the story and the silent cut are approved.
- Regenerate the whole voice for one changed word.
- Use stock footage of strangers, a fake product screen or a redrawn logo.
- Post or publish the film. The founder does. Gates: `shared/gates.md` (core).
