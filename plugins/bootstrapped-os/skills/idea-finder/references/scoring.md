# Scoring: the scorecard, the kill rules, and this week's kill test

Score each wedge after the signals are in, never before. Every score cites its evidence by row number in the signal log. No evidence for a row = 0.

## The scorecard (0-3 each, max 18)
| Row | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | No one complains in their own words | Mild annoyance | Clear cost: hours, a client, money | A hard stop: "I tried, it rejects me", the product can't take money at all |
| **Frequency** | Once ever | Yearly | Monthly | Weekly, or at every payout / every new piece of work |
| **Money already spent** | Nothing | Time only | A cheap tool or a DIY workaround they maintain | A paid fix they resent: a company abroad, 8-9% fees, a freelancer |
| **Reach** | Can't name anyone | Know where they gather | Can name 20 | They already ask the founder directly |
| **Founder edge** | No link to the buyer | Understands the job | Did the job / is the buyer | Was the buyer, and has a working tool or their trust |
| **Build size** (reverse) | Needs a team, a licence or two sides at once | Months alone | Weeks alone | Can deliver by hand next week |

Read the total: **14-18** go to the brief · **10-13** a week of conversations, test the weakest row first · **under 10** park it, log why.

Never average away a 0 in **pain** or **money already spent**. Those two rows decide whether anyone pays.

## Kill rules (any one = kill)
1. **No linked signal:** fewer than 3 A/B signals from different people after a real search, with the searched table to prove it.
2. **No moment:** you can't say when it hurts.
3. **Nobody pays anything today:** no money, no time, no workaround.
4. **Can't reach 20 people.**
5. **Price-only gap:** "same, cheaper".
6. **Needs both sides at once:** a marketplace that's worthless until it's big.
7. **Needs permission first:** a licence, a regulator, or a partner's approval before the first sale.
8. **The pitch attacks a supplier you depend on.** Name the access, not the gatekeeper.
9. **The founder doesn't want these customers** for two years of weekly talks.
10. **All signals are stale:** nothing in the last 12 months, or nothing under 60 days to contact.
11. **Only builders want it:** every signal is from people building in the space, not buying. (His broad version: interest from people excited about the trend, zero buyers.)

Write each result in the brief: "passed" with the evidence, or "killed" with the reason.

## This week's kill test (pick one, write the number down first)
**A. The one-sentence email (his "Switch" test).** 30 emails to people already paying for a workaround, one sentence naming their workaround and the better fix. The shape: "You're doing [job] through [workaround]. We do it properly, and we'll move you over for free." **5 replies means the wedge is real. 1 means it isn't, and you learned that for free.** Find the 30 among people already paying for a workaround: public posts, startup directories, people who asked the founder before ("thirty names is a day").

**B. The demand post (his first-paying-customer post).** One post that shares a real number and ends with one question: "tell me in one line what's stopping you." Read the replies, not the likes:

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 8+ people describe a specific stuck point | Run it. That's cohort one |
| Lots of likes, few specifics | An audience, not a market. Don't build yet |
| Under 3 replies | Wrong audience or wrong day. Try once more before quitting |
| Someone asks "how do I build the app?" | Not your buyer. Don't invite them |

Then send the offer only to the people who replied with a real stuck point. That tests demand, not distribution.

**What doesn't count as passing:** reactions, follows, sign-ups, "send me the guide". His April run got plenty of replies, guide requests and one call, then made $0. Replies are not customers.

## Ties and judgement
- On a tie, pick the higher **reach**, then the higher **founder edge**. The speed of the first 20 conversations beats elegance.
- A high score with one row at 0: test that row first, in the first conversations.
- If the founder's own data (who paid) disagrees with the web signals, the data wins. Re-score.

## What not to score
Market-size (TAM) estimates, trend charts, "AI is growing", search-volume guesses, a funded competitor's price as a target. For a solo founder, his reference point is trustmrr.com/stats (Sep 2026): 67.8% of verified startups are under $1k a month. So the first goal is a handful of payers, and the scorecard measures exactly that.
