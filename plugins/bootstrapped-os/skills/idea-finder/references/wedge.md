# Wedge: from a category to one painful moment

A wedge is the smallest group with the sharpest version of the problem that the founder can reach this week. Win them, then widen.

## 1. The ladder (narrow one rung at a time)
His real narrowing, 2026:

| Rung | Broad version (April) | Wedge (August) |
|---|---|---|
| Category | Payments for software agents and digital money | Getting paid by customers abroad |
| Segment | "Creators, solopreneurs, indie hackers, small businesses, founders in the region": 5 profiles in his own customer doc | Solo makers and small teams in countries the big card processors don't serve, often with no company |
| Job | "Accept payments" | Charge foreign customers by card, legally, and get the money into a local bank account |
| Moment | None | The day a processor rejects them, or tells them to open a company abroad first |

**Wedge line:** "[who] who [job], at [moment], when [what goes wrong]." If it needs "and", it is two wedges.

The words that ended up on his homepage were the buyers' own: "No company needed." That phrase came from the question people asked him again and again, not from a copywriter.

## 2. Count what they said, not what you pitched
Run this the moment the founder has calls, forms, emails or bookings. Sort every reason a buyer gave into one of four levels:

| Evidence level | What counts |
|---|---|
| **Stated** | Their own words: an email, a booking note they typed, their own site copy |
| **Stated (weak)** | A call-note paraphrase attributed to them, or a message relayed by someone else |
| **Inferred** | Your deduction from facts (no tool live, a question left unanswered) |
| **Not counted** | Anything the founder pitched on the call, or the booking page's own description |

Then count each reason at each level. An invented example, for a bookkeeping tool for freelance designers:

| Reason | Stated | With paraphrase | With inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invoices go out late because nobody chases them | 7 | 9 | 14 |
| Can't see what's owed at a glance | 4 | 5 | 11 |
| Tax-time panic (the founder's headline pitch) | 1 | 1 | 9 |
| "My accountant asked for it" | 0 | 0 | 2 |

The pitched reason only looks big once you add your own inferences. What buyers say first is chasing invoices, so the headline moves to that.

Two more things to look for in the same count:
- **Files with no reason at all.** If many buyers never said why they came, you can't count them. The fix is a required field on the sign-up or booking form (below).
- **What the alternative really is.** In some markets it isn't "doing nothing" but another tool that already works. A wedge is only real where the alternative is a bad fix.

### The field that makes the next count possible
Add it to the founder's sign-up or booking form, multi-select plus one free line:

> **Why [product] instead of [the usual alternative], or doing it yourself?**
> - [The usual alternative] isn't available to me, or rejected me
> - I need [the outcome] and can't get it today
> - I don't have [the thing the alternative requires]
> - I don't want to handle [the chore]
> - My customers want [the thing]
> - Other: ____

Re-run the counts monthly.

## 3. Follow the money, then the moment
| Look at | What it tells you | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Sign-ups vs payers | Sign-ups are cheap; real payments are rare at first | Stop treating sign-ups as demand |
| Where first sales came from | Often a segment you didn't plan for (a country, an industry) | Make that segment the beachhead and the rest a lead source |
| A free feature on the headline | Whether anyone uses it, in their words | If almost nobody does, demote it to a feature |
| Where the volume came from | Often a handful of customers of one kind | Open a lane for them: fixed packages, not custom quotes |

With `bootstrapped-core` connected, ask `evidence` (topic: idea) for the author's measured numbers behind each row.

The services lesson became a path. In his words: "that's the path": **service -> productized package -> tool -> SaaS.** It is his own story (a consultancy became the product). A freelancer selling custom quotes is not a wedge for a product. The same freelancer selling "a landing page, up to 5 sections, fixed price, 10 days, 1 revision" is.

## 4. Five tests a wedge must pass
1. **20 names.** Real handles, threads or companies. If you find 5 in an hour, 20 exist.
2. **Money already spent on a bad fix.** His buyers paid in money and hours: a company abroad with a yearly fee, 8-9% to receive dollars, a relative's account, invoices typed by hand.
3. **Reachable this week.** A community the founder is in, people who already asked him, a national subreddit. "Run ads" is not reach. A third-party study of first-customer stories (lead-scorer.com, 2023-2026, not the author's data) found almost none came from ads.
4. **The moment repeats.** His wedge hits at sign-up and then on every payout and tax question.
5. **One person can build it in weeks**, or deliver it by hand first.

## 5. The gap test (vs incumbents)
1. Name the 2-3 fixes people use today, including "doing nothing" and "a spreadsheet". Read each one's live pricing page and note the price and the date.
2. Read their newest 1-3 star reviews and "rejected / suspended" posts (the queries are in `signals.md`).
3. Find the segment inside the complaints: "works for X, but for us...". His was the countries an incumbent's own country list leaves out, checked on the incumbent's page in June 2026, not from memory.
4. Write the gap in one line: "[incumbent type] works for [who it's built for]; [our segment] says it fails at [moment] because [their words]."
5. Not a price gap. In his market, provider fees run from about 4% to 10%, and he called competing on price "a race to the bottom... zero is zero". "Too expensive" alone is not a gap.

## 6. Framing traps he hit
| Trap | What happened | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning against your own supplier | A line aimed at a supplier's rejected customers, while the product runs on that same supplier, reads as getting around them | Name the access, not the gatekeeper |
| A trend instead of a buyer | "It's the future, but nobody needed it yet" (his verdict on the agent-payments version) | A wedge needs a buyer today |
| A list of profiles | 5 buyer types in the customer doc | Pick the one with the most stated, paid signals |
| Supply-side data as the buyer | A buyer's city guessed from where the sellers were | Buyer-side claims need buyer-side evidence |

## 7. Patterns behind his wedges (search directions, not ideas)
- A tool built for one set of countries, needed by people outside it. (His wedge.)
- A big tool built for teams, used by solo people who need one part.
- A service people pay for monthly with the same steps every time: package it first, then make it a tool.
- A recent rule or platform change that left people scrambling. Date it and link the source.

Each one still needs its own linked signals.
