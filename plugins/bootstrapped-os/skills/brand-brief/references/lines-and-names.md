# Lines, names and words: the author's real rounds, with his verdicts

> Real, 2026. These come from the pack author's two brands: a payments product (July-October) and a founder community (September-October). Dates are when each line was tried. Quotes in the verdict column are his own words. Use the tables as tests, not as lines to copy.

## 1. The payments product's hero line, round by round
| Date | Line | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 16 Jul | "One platform, from first invoice to payout." | Rejected the next day as vague |
| 17 Jul | "Get paid where the big platforms won't" | Rejected as vague |
| 17 Jul | "Get paid by clients worldwide. Keep almost all of it." | Kept: who pays, what you keep |
| Jul | "Turned down by [processor]? We've got you" | **Dropped**: it was aimed at a supplier's rejected customers while the product ran on that same supplier |
| 1 Aug | Access-first: every processor and rival name removed from the homepage (a grep count of 0) | The message is access, not who turned you down |
| 2 Aug | "Sell globally. Unlock your revenue." | Kept for a month |
| 24 Aug | "Built to grow your revenue", after about 8 rounds of a chart drawn under the headline | "good idea, terrible execution", then "KISS" |
| 24 Sep | "Take card payments in 24 hours. Save $1,000. Earn more." | Replaced the same day after research |
| 24 Sep | "Turn users into paying customers." | Kept as the promise |
| 26 Sep | "Payments, tax and pricing for SaaS." | His reason: the hero was "too abstract" next to rivals who name the category in their first words |
| 1 Oct | "Sell globally. Grow from anywhere." (replaced "Let the whole world pay you.") | He wanted a mantra as recognisable as a rival's two-line one, "but not copied" |
| 1 Oct | Plain promise, after studying a competitor's hero | "No one says MoR... we need to lead with that": say what you do in the buyer's words |
| 12 Oct (live) | "Sell digital products and services by card or monthly. For freelancers, agencies and SaaS, with customers in 190+ countries. No company needed." | Who, what and the buyers' own phrase. It held |

**What held:** the lines that used the buyers' words ("No company needed", "get paid by clients") lasted. The ones built on his feature names or on abstractions lasted a day.

## 2. The community's line, round by round
| Date | Line | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 14 Sep | "$0 to $1k MRR in 30 days." (result + timeframe + a named group of 100 founders) | Kept for a month; every number needed a visible source |
| 17 Sep | Audience narrowed to "agency owners and high-earning freelancers building their first product": "You have revenue. The product has zero customers." | Kept as a hook |
| 9 Oct | "I help bootstrapped founders go from $0 to paying customers" | A journey, not a thing you get |
| 13 Oct | "I help bootstrapped founders get their first paying customers." | Concrete, but too narrow once the offer grew |
| 14 Oct | "The business half of your startup, done with you" | **Rejected:** "I need to understand what you are doing in the first 3 seconds" |
| 14 Oct | "Builders build. Makers get paid." + the plain sentence directly under it | **Kept**: a playful line only works with a plain one under it |
| 17 Oct | "free money" for grants and AI credits | **Rejected:** "it looks scammy". Now "grants and AI credits, no equity" |

## 3. Names: tried, rejected, kept
| Thing being named | Tried | Verdict | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| A free lead magnet | "Price Prism", "Price X-ray", "Mystery Shop" | "jargon, people don't know what it is" | The visitor's own question: **"Why isn't anyone paying?"**, button "Find out why" |
| The AI feature | "pricing agent" | "Agent" is an internal word | **"Pricing Intelligence"**; check the live site before naming anything |
| A category label | "agentic Merchant of Record" | Its page got almost no visits. "Don't confuse users, one label only" | One label: "growth Merchant of Record", in titles and meta only |
| A launch hook | "Waze for ..." (a rival's), "Lighthouse for ..." | Waze was taken by someone else's launch. **He didn't know what Lighthouse was, so it fails recognisability** | "Shazam for your first sale", tested in posts, never on the site |
| A hook ending | "... for sales", "... for revenue" | "For sales" reads as a sales-team tool; "for revenue" is abstract | "... for your first sale" |
| The community itself | "membership group", "group", "room", "WhatsApp group" | Struck: it reads as a cheap chat group | **"community platform"**, exactly |
| The paid tier | "more playbooks" | "fix the problem that membership is a playbook; it's an access" | Access to something that happens to *your* product |
| A product inside the community | "my-ai-team"; "Get [product]", "the OS in five parts" | The product framed as the whole offer | A product name plus "a product of [community]; members get access" |
| A hire in that product | "Free money" | Scammy | **"Grants & credits"** |
| The domain | A country-code domain | Moved to the full name on `.com` | A `.com` with the full name |

## 4. Words: replace on sight
| Don't write | Write | His reason or the run |
|---|---|---|
| "free" (on a paid brand) | "$0", or name the exchange: "for an email" | "Free" cheapens it and pulls the wrong people. $0 was already in the logo |
| "free money" | "grants and AI credits, no equity"; "non-dilutive funding" in labels | "it looks scammy" |
| "cheapest" | "the lowest real cost" | Reads cheap. "Cheapest" only in question-matched titles and FAQs, where the searcher typed it |
| "Merchant of Record", "settlement", "stablecoin", "VAT", "IBAN" in the hero | "official seller", "lands in your bank", "digital dollars", "sales tax", "bank account number" | "I didn't understand anything here, just cool buzzwords" |
| "no company abroad" | "no need to open a company in another country" | A cold reader doesn't know the backstory |
| A supplier's or rival's name on the homepage | The outcome without the name | Rival names live on "vs" pages only |
| "Get started", "Learn more", "Book a demo" (for self-serve) | A verb plus what they get: "Find out why", "Try for $0" | "Book a demo" is enterprise language |
| "No more guessing.", tagline tails, kickers that repeat the headline | Delete | "measure words as people are doom scrolling" |
| "membership group", "room" | "community platform" | See names |
| A transliterated English word in the second-language site | The native word, checked against official terms | His second-language "playbook": the transliteration was rejected as an anglicism; the native word for "guide" was rejected because the tax office's official guide uses it |

## 5. The messaging layers (approved 29 Sep 2026: "remember it, it's good")
| Layer | What it is | Where it appears | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Brand | The name | Everywhere | Never |
| 2 Promise | One outcome line | Homepage H1, link-preview image, bios, video end card | Yearly |
| 3 Category | What it is, in search words | Titles, meta, comparison pages, llms.txt, legal. **Never as a hook** | Rarely |
| 4 Products | Plain names: "[what] + noun" or "[brand] for [who]" | Nav, features, docs, pricing | When shipped |
| 5 Hooks | "X for Y" analogies | One post, video or campaign. Never in the nav, never as a name | Every launch, disposable |

The rule that came out of it: mixing a hook, a product name and a category in one sentence "is what made it feel like jargon". The site uses layers 1-4; hooks live in posts. When the promise must cover several audiences, keep it broad on the homepage and narrow it per audience on subpages.

## 6. Tests every line passes
1. **3 seconds:** a stranger can say what it is, for whom, and what they get.
2. **Competitor page:** if the line could sit unchanged on a rival's page, it's too vague.
3. **Category in the first words**, or right under a playful line.
4. **The buyers' phrase:** at least one word or phrase from a linked quote ("No company needed").
5. **Concrete:** a transformation (X to Y) or a verified digit beats a topic. "What actually gets paid" was rejected as a headline because it's a topic.
6. **Can you picture it?** "Then we fix your pricing" was rejected ("this tells nothing!"). "We test your product and checkout, suggest a pricing strategy, and keep watching your pricing and churn" was approved: verbs and objects.
7. **True today:** every number is real and sourced, with no tilde ("$3,600 in March", never "~$3.6k"). Say what the number counts.

## 7. The story behind the brand-brief steps
- **One moment (step 3).** His community mixed three wedges into "distribution, content, customers and non-dilutive funding". It was honest, but it had no single moment, and he spent the next week ordering it (funding first, then AI agents, then revenue).
- **The promise line (step 4).** His lasting payments line came from what people kept asking ("No company needed"), not from his feature list.
- **A plain sentence under a playful line (step 5).** "Builders build. Makers get paid." only passed with the plain sentence under it. "The business half of your startup, done with you" had none and failed.
- **Naming rules (step 7).**
  - A reference must be one the founder already knows. "Lighthouse for ..." failed because he didn't know Lighthouse. An analogy someone else just launched with ("Waze for ...") is out.
  - A free tool is named with the visitor's question, never an invented term. "Price Prism" lost to "Why isn't anyone paying?".
  - One label per thing. A second category label he coined got almost no visits and was retired.
  - A word that cheapens it is banned in the name and the copy ("group", "room" for a community platform).
  - Before naming a feature, read the live site. The name buyers see wins over the internal one.
  - If the audience is international, prefer the full name on `.com` over a country code. His community moved from a country-code domain to its full name on `.com`.
- **Words (step 8).** On a paid brand, "free" becomes "$0" or "for an email". "Free money" is never used ("it looks scammy").
- **Voice (step 9).** His three words: blunt, plain, outcome-first.
- **Palette (step 10).** "I don't like many colors." A second colour only for one named job (his cyan: live markers only). His yellow is 1.1:1 on white, so it is a fill with navy text on top (16.0:1). He called purple glow on white "AI slop". Changing the accent later is a rebrand, not a tweak. A sibling brand by the same founder may reuse the accent: both his brands use #4F46E5.
- **Fonts (step 11).** His payments site used Bricolage Grotesque 500 with Inter for the body. He called the display font "overused" once it spread to labels.
- **Second language (step 13).** His second-language "playbook": the transliteration was rejected as an anglicism, and the native word for "guide" collided with his tax office's official guide.
- **Approved lines stay (step 15).** He had to restore an approved subhead an assistant rewrote an hour later: "why did you remove the subsection it was nice."
