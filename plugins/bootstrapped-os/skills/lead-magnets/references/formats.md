# Lead-magnet formats (each with the author's own)

Pick one. Build it in whatever the founder already uses (a doc, a notes page, a site page). Don't buy a tool for the first one.

| Format | Fits when the buyer asks | They hold at the end | You hold back | Capture | The author's |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Playbook | "How do I…?" | An output on their own business | The case-by-case answers, the full rules, your sources | Step 1 public, the rest after a free sign-up; or the link by DM after "yes" | "What to charge before anyone has paid" (free); a get-paid-from-abroad guide (by DM) |
| Audit teaser | "Is mine OK?" | 1-2 findings on their own thing, with the fix | The other findings, the full report, the plan | Reply, then by hand | A pricing and checkout audit: sign up like a customer, test the page and checkout, a written report |
| Calculator page | "How much / how long?" | Their number from their inputs | What to do with it | Free, ungated | Fee calculators per pair of providers, framed as "how much lands in your bank" |
| Swipe file | "Can I see real ones?" | Real examples with what to copy | The review of theirs | Free, ungated | Bootstrapped SaaS landing pages: headline, button, cheapest plan, each checked live with the date |
| Dataset | "Who covers / costs what?" | The table and its download | The interpretation for their case | Free, CC BY, a link back asked | Payment providers across 85 countries, on Hugging Face and Kaggle |
| Checklist | "What should I check?" | A pass/fail list they ran themselves | What to do about each "no" | Email, or free as proof | Each layer of his pricing playbook ends with a pass condition |

## Playbook
- **Shape:** "By the end you'll have…" (their outcome), then 4-6 steps. Each step: the question -> a real example's answer and what it showed -> **"Your turn:"** the same question on the reader's product. Ends with a worksheet or a first action.
- **His pricing playbook:** six layers (the model, the number, the offer and three more), each with a pass condition, run on a real app, then the reader's worksheet. 5 minutes.
- **Length:** ~5 minutes = about 1,150-1,250 words at 230 words a minute. Compute it. His 14-minute first draft was cut to 5.
- **Write it for any country.** No local tax office names, no local banks: tax becomes "the questions to ask your accountant" plus an illustrative example.
- **Common miss (he caught it):** conclusions about the founder's product instead of a process the reader applies to theirs. "People love to read, resonate and process so they apply on their product, not conclusions for me."

## Audit teaser
- **Shape:** one page. What you saw as a buyer, dated. The most expensive finding, one screenshot. One more finding. A named list of what the full audit covers, without the answers. One button.
- **His two findings that made the hook:** a free tier giving 10 times what a real user needs; a "Get Pro" button that took 3 clicks to reach checkout.
- **Every figure is verified (you saw it) or arithmetic** from verified figures. No "+30% revenue" on a business whose numbers you haven't seen.

## Calculator page
- 2-4 inputs, one result, the formula in plain words underneath. Only if the maths is simple and true.
- **His reframe that made it useful:** first-year total cost including time and the company you'd need, not just the fee. The answer readers want is what lands in their bank.
- Be honest when a rival wins. His fee pages say which provider is cheaper per sale, and win on what his product does that theirs doesn't.

## Swipe file
- Each entry checked on the live site, with the date, the headline, the button text, the cheapest plan, and one line on what to copy.
- It answers a search nobody else answers well (his: no bootstrapped-specific list ranked), so it doubles as a page AI assistants cite.

## Dataset
- Publish the table you built for your own work, with a date, a licence (CC BY) and a source line. Post the one headline number from it ("isn't available in 37 of 85 countries").
- Data is what other people link to. A listicle can't copy it.

## Naming it
- The title is the outcome in the buyer's words: "What to charge before anyone has paid", "Get paid for your product without opening a company".
- No "Ultimate", "Secret", "Hack", "Blueprint", "Masterclass".
- One line under the title: who it's for and how many minutes.
