# Handing it out: posts and DMs

A magnet nobody sees does nothing. Order: **people who already know the founder first, by hand. Then a post. Then strangers who showed the problem.** You draft the post and list who to send it to, with why each person fits. The founder writes and sends every DM in their own words, a few at a time. The shapes below are patterns for the founder, not scripts to paste.

## The proof post (LinkedIn)
The post is the hook for the magnet, not the magnet. It proves; it doesn't teach the whole method.

```
[Line 1: what happened, with the real number. 2-8 words if possible.]

[2-4 short lines: the before, what changed, the after.]

[1 line: what the reader can do with it.]

I wrote it up as [format] on [outcome in plain words]. About [N] minutes.

[Soft close: "link in the first comment" or "write to me and I'll send it".]
```
- Link in the **first comment**, not the body.
- One proof per post. A second proof is a second post on another day.
- No "Comment GUIDE below". Say what's in it and where it is.
- Founders with a home-language or local network: post the local news in that language to that network. It may beat English by a lot (see `voice-example.md`).

Other post shapes that carry a magnet well:
- **Founder-pattern observation:** "I talked to [N] founders this month. Almost all [did X]." Only if it's true and counted. Then the one fix, then "the full checklist is here".
- **Launch post:** the real launch result, a thank-you, then "today I'm publishing the first playbook, on [question in the buyer's words]".

## The X version (3 lines)
```
[the number, what happened]
[the one lesson]
wrote up the steps: [outcome]. link below.
```

## The DM (warm people first)
Before the founder writes, check the thread: never message someone twice, never cold-pitch a friend (`sales-outreach` keeps the list).

**Connection note** (if needed): about 10 words, no pitch, no guide. "thanks for liking the post; would love to connect." The magnet comes in the DM after they accept. The author sent a longer version for months; after a batch he read them back as an AI sales pitch and cut them.

**The ask** (pattern):
```
hey [name]. [one line about them: their post, their question, how you know them].
wrote a free [format] on [outcome in plain words].
want me to send it?
```
**After "yes":**
```
hey [name]. as promised, here's the [format] on [outcome]: [link]
```
**Warm network, sent directly** (people who know the founder and have the problem):
```
hey [name]. you asked me about [their problem] a while ago.
I wrote up what worked for me: [outcome]. [link]
no need to reply, hope it saves you some time.
```

### The one-sentence test
After reading the DM, can the person tell a friend in one sentence what they'd get? "A guide on how to accept the payment and get euros in your own bank account" passes. "Documented the setup that finally worked" fails.

## Follow-ups
Use the one follow-up cadence in [`sales-outreach`](../../sales-outreach/SKILL.md#follow-ups). The founder says most of his paying customers came from follow-ups, not the first message (his statement). Each follow-up must add something new or close the loop.

**They replied:** answer their question first, give one next step, wait. If they say "that's me", name the paid offer and the price, straight.

## Email (if the magnet collects addresses)
- The form says what they get and how often you'll write ("one email a week, unsubscribe anytime"). The newsletter is its own unticked checkbox, not the price of the magnet.
- Email 1: a personal note from the founder, 40-80 words, one button to the magnet. No feature list, no price.
- Then the founder's normal newsletter. Every email has a visible unsubscribe.
- No surprise sequences. If you'll send 5 emails about the paid offer, say so on the form.
- The author's newsletter runs on Kit's free plan from his own domain. Every issue is also a public post, with 2-3 links to pages on his main site: Kit public posts are dofollow, so the newsletter builds the site's DR too.

## Track it
One line per person in `knowledge/customers.md`: who, how they got it (DM, post, form), date, follow-up due, what they said. Count weekly: sent, replied, asked about paying, paid.

## How the author does it
- **DMs got replies, not money.** His DM run (for an earlier product, a stablecoin wallet) got many replies, many guides sent and one call, and made €0. The guide got replies; the product didn't match a pain felt now. **Replies are not customers.**
- **The yeses were one word:** "sure", "please share", "yes sure", "tell me more". Make yes that easy.
- **The vague message:** "documented the setup that finally worked…". The reply: "I'm not quite sure what you're offering." The rewrite named the outcome: a free guide on how to accept the payment and get euros in your own bank account, "want me to send it?"
- **Links in posts get almost no clicks.** A year of posts brought huge impressions and very few link clicks. The post builds trust; the DM and the follow-up do the handing out.
- **The launch post that carried a magnet:** "2 days after I launched [community], [N] have signed up. Today I'm publishing the first handbook, which answers: I built a product, now what? Can I get paid from abroad as a private person, without a company?" Then three lines on what's inside and "sign up to get it".
