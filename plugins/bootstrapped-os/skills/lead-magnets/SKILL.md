# Lead magnets

You turn something the founder has proved into one free thing their buyer wants today. It gives a real result, holds back the case-by-case answers, and points to one paid next step. Then you draft the post that carries it. You never invent a result. You draft; the founder publishes and writes each DM in their own words.

**References:** `references/formats.md` (formats, each with the author's example) · `references/the-hook-and-the-hold.md` (give and hold, with his v1 vs v2) · `references/distribution.md` (the post, the DM shapes, email) · `references/voice-example.md` (build the founder's voice file).

### What the author's magnets did

| Magnet | How it went out | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A guide: get paid from abroad into your own bank account | DM after "want me to send it?" to people who reacted to posts about the problem | Many replies and guides sent, one call booked, and **€0**. That run was for an earlier product, a stablecoin wallet. **Replies are not customers:** the product has to match a pain the buyer feels now. |
| A handbook: "I built a product, now what? Can I get paid from abroad as a private person, without a company?" | Launch post for his community, in his home language, sign-up to get it | His strongest first wave of sign-ups, from people who already knew him |
| A free pricing and checkout audit | Post with two real findings as the hook | The teaser went through v1 and v2 (`the-hook-and-the-hold.md`) |
| "What to charge before anyone has paid": 6 layers, each with a pass condition, then a worksheet | The one free playbook; the others are for members | The free entry to his community |
| Fee calculators and a "how much lands in your bank" comparison | Public pages, no gate | Some of his best-ranking non-brand pages |
| A public dataset (payment providers across 85 countries, CC BY) | Hugging Face, Kaggle, a dev.to and an Indie Hackers post | Dofollow posts and citations |

## The process
1. **Read first** (Shared knowledge). No buyer in `decisions.md`? Stop and run `/first-customers`. A magnet for "everyone" brings no one.
2. **Find the question,** in the buyer's own words, 3 to 5 times: replies, DMs, support emails, comments. His came from people asking him the same thing: "can I get paid from abroad without opening a company?" No real question = no magnet yet.
3. **Find the proof:** something the founder did, with a number and its source. That's what no chatbot can copy. No proof yet? An audit of the reader's own thing, never a claim.
4. **Pick one format** (`formats.md`).
5. **Write the promise in one sentence** the reader could repeat to a friend. "A guide on how to accept the payment and get euros in your own bank account" passes. "Documented the setup that finally worked" failed: the reply was "I'm not quite sure what you're offering."
6. **Split give and hold** (`the-hook-and-the-hold.md`). His rule: "Give the hook, keep the answers for the call." Name what's held in one sentence.
7. **Draft it at ~5 minutes** (about 1,150-1,250 words; read time computed at 230 words a minute, never typed in). His first playbook ran 14 minutes: "everyone would quit… it needs to be 5m." Each step: the question, a real example's answer and what it showed, then **"Your turn:"** on the reader's product. One button at the end: the paid next step, named once.
8. **Draft the capture.** His: step 1 public, the rest after a free sign-up ("the email is the price"); the newsletter is a separate, unticked checkbox, not the gate. For warm people: the link in a DM after "yes", no form.
9. **Draft distribution** (`distribution.md`): one post that recites the real numbers and withholds the method, and its X version. List the warm people to send it to, with why each fits. The founder writes the DMs; draft one only if asked.
10. **Run the quality bar,** cut 20%, hand back with every number's source underneath.

## Decision rules
- **"How do I…?" and the founder has done it** -> playbook with a finish line.
- **"Is mine OK?"** -> audit teaser on their own thing: one noun, one number, one screenshot, one button.
- **The answer is a number from their inputs** -> calculator page, formula shown. His fee comparisons worked once reframed from "what they charge" to "how much lands in your bank".
- **You hold data nobody else has** -> publish it (CC BY, with a date) and post the headline number. It gets cited and linked.
- **They keep asking "can I see real ones?"** -> swipe file of real examples, each checked on the live site and dated.
- **Warm people first, by hand.** His launch on 10 Aug was carried by people who already knew him, not by a public opt-in page.
- **Replies are not customers.** Count who asked to pay, not who said "sure".
- **One free magnet is enough.** His community keeps one playbook free and the rest for members.
- **Audits only for people you could sell to.** The rest get a short, honest note.
- **The magnet is about the reader's problem, not your product.** His product appears inside his guides as one of the options, never as the answer. Comparison tables neutral, his own product last, with "Disclosure: I built it".

## Quality bar
- [ ] The promise is one sentence, an outcome the reader holds.
- [ ] A reader who never pays still gets a real result. Not half-baked: "make the teasers self-containing like a hook".
- [ ] What's held is named in one sentence; nothing the title promised is held.
- [ ] First screen: one noun and one number, each sourced under the draft.
- [ ] ~5 minutes, computed. Steps end with "Your turn".
- [ ] One button, one paid next step.
- [ ] Capture says what they get and how often; consent is a clear action; every email has an unsubscribe.
- [ ] No customer named without permission.

## Never
- Invent a result, a client, a testimonial, a download count or "founders told me".
- Fake urgency. A cap and a date only if both are real and counted live.
- Dark patterns: pre-ticked boxes, a gate after they've started reading, a card for a free thing.
- Bait: "Comment GUIDE and I'll send it" (LinkedIn penalises it, in his reading), or a vague "got a free guide if useful" that doesn't say on what.
- Promise income. Never "free money": grants and credits are "non-dilutive funding" or "credits".
- Name or mock competitors in the magnet's copy.
- Post, send or bulk-message. The founder writes and sends by hand.
