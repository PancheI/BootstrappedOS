# First paid customers: the offer and the route

Every audit for a founder at $0 answers two questions: **what to charge first** and **where the first paying customers come from.** Pick one offer and one route from the tables below. Put the grade next to any figure you quote. Quote shapes, never targets.

**Grades:** **Strong** = a dataset with a stated sample. **Medium** = several founder self-reports or a vendor dataset. **Thin** = one or two cases. **Opinion** = a practitioner's view, no data.

Researched 22 Sep 2026. Re-open a source before quoting it; if it has moved, say so. The Lead Scorer survey had 70 founders; the smaller n in a line is the share of them who answered that question.

## What the evidence says, in seven lines
1. **The first paying customer often arrives about two weeks after launch.** Median 14 days (n = 24 solo founders, Lead Scorer, 9 Aug 2026) and about 14 days across 21 public case studies (Indie Hackers interviews, Jun-Jul 2026). $1k MRR took 1 to 12 months. Fast first payment is common; fast growth is not. *Medium, survivor data.*
2. **Someone else's audience is the biggest source of first payers.** Of 59 founders who named a channel: someone else's community 34% (Reddit alone 25%), own audience 20%, one-to-one cold outreach 15%, Product Hunt and marketplaces 10%, SEO 8%, warm network 7%, paid ads 0 (Lead Scorer, 9 Aug 2026). In the case studies, borrowed distribution (one partner who already reaches the buyer) covered every fast result without its own audience. *Medium.*
3. **Hand-picked outreach works; volume doesn't.** Lists under 50 people: 14-58% reply. Scraped lists over 300: 1-1.6% (Lead Scorer, 15 founder campaigns). Average across 7.5M cold emails in 2025: 0.45% (Belkins). *Medium.*
4. **Paid ads, paid "featured" directory slots and affiliate programs don't produce first customers.** Ads: 0 of 66 first customers (Lead Scorer). Featured slots: no documented case of payback. Affiliates: 1.28% of affiliates ever make a sale (Rewardful, 2,847 programs, 2026). *Medium.*
5. **Asking for the card up front converts about five times better** in trials: ~30% vs roughly 6% (Growth Unhinged / ChartMogul / ProductLed, 200 B2B products, Jan 2026; the 6% is derived from "more than 5x"). The cost is fewer sign-ups and refunds from people who forget, so send a reminder before the charge. *Strong, but the sample is $1-10M ARR companies, not pre-revenue.*
6. **Cheap plans lose their buyers.** AI products under $50/month keep 23% of revenue after a year; above $250/month, 70% (ChartMogul, 3,500 companies, Dec 2025). In the case studies, both documented price rises improved results (one Reddit-marketing tool cut its cheap tiers: trial-to-paid 30% to 52%, churn 43% to 25%; Indie Hackers, 16 Jul 2026). *Strong on the problem, thin on the fix.*
7. **Much popular advice has no evidence behind it.** Founding-member deals, money-back guarantees, regional pricing and decoy tiers rest on vendor claims or anecdotes; the decoy effect failed to replicate. Lifetime deals bring fast cash and were dropped by the one founder who reported what followed. Never present these as proven.

## The offer: what to charge first

| The product | Start with | Why | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B or prosumer SaaS, clear job, $20-250/month | **14-day trial, card up front**, one or two flat plans, a reminder 3 days before the charge | Highest measured conversion; payment happens without a second decision | Strong |
| Same, but buyers won't give a stranger a card, or it touches their data | **No-card trial with a setup call**, then ask for the card inside the product | Trust first; conversion rides on onboarding | Medium |
| Low ticket (under ~$30/month), consumer or prosumer | **Paid from day one, no free tier**, plus a yearly option | Cheap monthly plans churn fastest; yearly brings the cash forward and turns 12 card fees into one | Strong on the problem |
| AI generation tool | **Monthly plan with an included allowance, plus one-off top-up packs** | Covers usage cost; packs catch buyers who refuse subscriptions (one developer-API founder: +$500-700/month per product; Indie Hackers, 28 Jul 2026) | Medium trend, thin on speed |
| B2B, over ~$5k a year, needs setup | **A paid pilot close to full price** with written success criteria | The first invoice lands at kickoff; free pilots become endless trials | Opinion |
| Tool for local businesses | **Done-for-you setup fee plus a monthly fee** | Too few buyers for a low-converting free tier; owners buy help, not software | Opinion, one case |
| Service sold as "let's talk" | **2-3 fixed packages**: one outcome, counted contents, one price each, a buy button | Buyers can say yes without a call; see `models-and-break-even.md` | Opinion |
| Has an audience or waitlist, launching now | **A founding offer with a real cap and a real date**, card required, built as a paid trial or a locked price, one price line everywhere | Turns existing demand into cash. It doesn't create demand. The author's seven rounds of his own: `how-the-author-does-it.md` §3 | Opinion, plus one documented case |

## The route: where the first ten come from

| Product and buyer | Route 1 | Route 2 | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B SaaS for non-technical small businesses or agencies | **30 hand-picked people a day, one to one**, named buyer, written for them | Warm network, then posts with DMs to whoever engages | Medium |
| Developer tool or API | **Show HN** | Answer threads in developer communities; docs and comparison pages later | Thin to medium |
| Prosumer or AI productivity tool | **Answer existing threads about the exact problem** in buyer communities (not founder communities) | The marketplace that is the product's natural home (browser extension store, app marketplace) | Medium |
| Tool for founders and makers | **Posts, then a one-to-one DM to whoever engages** (reach alone rarely turns into clicks; the conversation does) | Product Hunt, **only on top of an audience and a working checkout** | Medium |
| Consumer mobile app | Short-form video, problem first | App store search | Thin to medium |
| E-commerce add-on | **The platform's app store** | One-to-one outreach to store owners | Thin |
| Local business tool | **The person owners already trust** (accountant, association, supplier, installer) on a recurring commission | Calls, visits, local groups | Opinion |
| No audience, any category | **Borrow one:** one partner who reaches the buyer, paid a share of revenue (one Upwork-lead tool: $10k MRR in 60 days via one coach; one seller-research tool: $10k MRR at day 30 via one seller community; Indie Hackers, Jun-Jul 2026) | Hand-picked outreach | Medium |

**Small market? You don't have to sell locally.** The founders in the case set who started in small markets got their first payers from a global niche, through an existing marketplace or partner.

**Not for the first ten:** paid ads, paid featured directory slots, affiliate programs, bulk directory submission, SEO, getting cited by AI search. The last two are real, but they're for customers 10 to 1,000 and take months.

## How it appears in the audit
- **One** offer and **one** route, each with its grade, and the first concrete action ("Monday: list 30 [buyers] you can name, write to 10"). No channel plan.
- If the founder's current plan is ads, paid slots or affiliates, say plainly the evidence says it won't bring the first ten.
- The daily work belongs to `/first-customers` (the sales-outreach skill): real people with a real, linked signal, one message each, drafts only.
