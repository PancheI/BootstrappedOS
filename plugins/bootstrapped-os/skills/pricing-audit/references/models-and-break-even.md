# Models and break-even

The audit is the models you would build for this product, with numbers. Not a list of what's broken on the page.

## 1. Before the models: change seats
What it costs you sets a floor, and a floor has never closed anybody. Answer the same product as the person asked to pay:

| | The buyer asks | Good enough when |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | What do I actually get? | An outcome they already wanted, repeatable after one read. Not a feature list |
| B2 | What else gets me that? | Named alternatives with real prices, **doing nothing always one of them** |
| B3 | What am I handing over, and for what? | The whole offer in one sentence, no asterisk |
| B4 | What do I get back? | A break-even in **their** unit they can count on one hand ("four extra bookings and it has paid for itself") |

B4 is the row audits keep missing. "$29 a month" is a cost; "one saved hour a week pays for it" is a decision. Compute it from the founder's own published numbers, and check the loss falls on the person who pays.

**Value for money:** list every item the pricing page sells. For each: can a buyer see or use it today, or is it only promised ("soon", no date, members-only and unseen)? Say in one line what the price buys for certain. Promised items are not delivered items.

## 2. Diagnose before you design
The author classifies the product first, in plain words on the page (never the framework's name):

| What you see | Plain words | The model it points to |
|---|---|---|
| A good product at $8-9 that does a job worth far more | "priced below what it does" | Yearly, or a higher plan with one capability more |
| Many features, nothing priced separately, value invisible | "everything in one box" | Pull out the feature buyers ask for as the paid line |
| One valuable feature buried in a bundle | "the best part is free" | Price that part (an add-on or the paid plan's reason to exist) |
| Nobody wants it at any price | "no buyer yet" | No model. The route to 10 conversations is the audit |
| Traffic that fits another model (a free tool with buying intent) | "money left on the table" | Affiliate, packs or a sponsor slot beside the product |

And look for the pattern the founder hasn't named: one product already metered several things a buyer uses (generations, exports, downloads) without calling them credits. That became "one credit bank, named actions", a repackaging, not a new build.

## 3. Design 2-3 models, each from a different angle
Shapes: a yearly price next to monthly · card-up-front trial · per-unit (seat, driver, project) · a bundled job at one price · allowance plus top-up packs · fixed service packages · "keep what you have, add X". Two models that differ only in the digit are one model.

For each model:
- **The offer in one line.**
- **The precedent:** a named product already charging this way, quoted from its live page, dated. A competitor that merely exists is not a precedent. Check it works the way you claim: a "first 500 customers, 6 left" counter that moved with every sale was a sales tactic, not a price step.
- **Why it fits:** from something the founder already has (a clause in their terms, a counter, a plan they show, a cost they carry). Turn problems into models ("charge the higher price your countdown already shows").
- **The numbers** (section 4).
- **Why it might be wrong, as a mechanism with a mitigation.** Not "this could have downsides":

| Model | The real risk | Mitigation the author uses |
|---|---|---|
| Credits or a visible balance | "Credit anxiety": a balance people can see suppresses use, even with plenty left | Name the action, not the unit ("one warmed comment = 10 credits"); top-ups at a posted price |
| A new top plan | It eats the plan below | Price it at the competitor's own top-to-entry ratio, read on their live page; differ by capability, not a bigger number of the same unit |
| Yearly | A buyer who'd have left in month 3 pays 9 months up front, then asks for a refund | Write what an early cancel gets back before the yearly plan ships |
| Card-up-front trial | Fewer sign-ups; refunds from people who forget | A reminder 3 days before the charge |
| Lifetime | Fast cash from the highest-intent buyers, then nothing | A capped number, next to the subscription, never instead of it |

**Usage or credits** only when all three hold: value or cost moves with use; the unit fits in a sentence a founder can say; starting is cheaper than a month of seats. A balance the buyer can't turn into a job ("170 credits" when it means 17 comments) is friction, not a model. Never on a $0 founder's first price.

## 4. The numbers
- **Baseline first:** the founder's verified figures (payment tool, public revenue page, counters, prices on every page). None public is normal at $0: say so; every figure after it is a projection.
- **Three rows per model:** base, other, risk, every input beside the result. Whole buyers, never fractions. **Both ends of a range** when the input is arguable (a token-per-slide range gives two answers; print both).
- **A "works if" line** per model: "works if 3 of your 16 monthly buyers take the year", "12 buyers at $29 cover your $340 a month". At $0 this line is the model's number.
- **The deciding assumption:** one sentence naming the input the ranking hangs on ("A earns more only if the yearly ask wins at least 2 in 3 as many buyers as the trial would").
- **Their $1,000 month:** $1,000 ÷ their price = N paying customers (arithmetic), next to check 6 in `evidence-checklist.md`.
- A round illustrative number is fine when labelled "assumption". A figure seen only in the code is not an input.

**Quick arithmetic**
- Yearly: monthly × months paid (read the precedents' live yearly prices; the core's `evidence` adds market figures when connected). Works if a buyer would have left before that month.
- Lifetime: lifetime ÷ monthly = months to break even; then what 24 months of monthly would have paid.
- Free-tier multiple: free allowance ÷ what a typical user actually uses.
- **Fees on a small price:** read the payment tool's live fee page, dated. Illustration with an assumed fee of 4.5% + €0.50 per card sale: €3 a month loses €0.64 (21%) per payment; €36 a year loses €2.12 (6%) once. Twelve fixed fees become one.

## 5. Am I profitable?
Every income stream (each plan, one-offs, services, add-ons; gross and net of fees, refunds and the tax the tool collects) and every cost (payment fees, hosting, AI usage per customer, tools, contractors, the founder's hours if wanted). Then: monthly net, cost to serve one customer, customers needed to cover costs (whole number). Sources from `numbers.md` or the payment tool. Booked is not paid.

## 6. Rank, pick, roll out
- **Ranked by money:** first-12-month value, base row, largest first. Different order? One clause why.
- **Pick one.** One alternative if the first stalls.
- **10 days, When | Do this, no effort estimates.** Current customers keep their price; any change is announced before it bills. At $0, the first week is the first move, then the route's first action.

**Landmines (at most two, only if present):** an uncapped lifetime or founding deal sold to the buyers with the most intent · a price on the page that doesn't open a real checkout (once payments exist) · freemium as the opener without the traffic to feed it · pay-before-trying when the product must touch the buyer's own data.

## 7. Services: the models are packages
A founder who sells only quoted work ("let's talk", "get a quote") gets 2-3 fixed packages built from services they already list:
- **One outcome** the buyer holds at the end: "Business profile set up and verified, 10 photos, 3 posts, a one-page report." "Growth" or "management" alone is not an outcome; if you can't say what the buyer receives, a payment company can't resell it.
- **Counted contents**, delivery window, revisions, what's not included, a durable digital deliverable (a report, a file, a login), one price that's the same for every buyer. Never "from" or "starting at". Mostly-live-calls packages are time sold by the hour, not a package.
- **"Every client is different"** is the objection every agency raises. Answer it on the page: the base package covers the typical client; what varies becomes priced add-ons, each a fixed item with its own buy button (a payment link carries one amount; never "picked at checkout"). Show one real client case from their own site rebuilt as base + add-ons, with the total. Truly one-off work stays a quote, billed outside the package checkout, named as the exception.
- **Break-even on their side:** hours per package × their hourly cost. ROI on the buyer's side. Priced against named packages similar providers publish, plus hire-in-house or DIY.
- **A ready menu:** one card per main service they list, the lead one recommended. First change: publish that package with a buy button.
