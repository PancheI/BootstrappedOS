# First customers in 30 days

For a live product with nobody paying yet. About 30 minutes a day. The agents find, check and count; the founder writes, talks and asks for the money.

## How the author did it (the run this plan is built from)
His payments product had an earlier version live for four months that made $0. The product wasn't the main problem: nobody knew it existed. Then he launched the new version on 10 Aug.

| When | What he did | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Before launch | A founding cohort with a hard cap and a fixed end date, posted to his own network in his home language | founders asked to join before launch day |
| Launch day (24 hours) | Posted the live funnel to the same network | visitors came, a share signed up, and the first two sales landed within the day. Most visitors were from his home country |
| The next days | Posted each real step (first users verified, a new way to pay added) | each post its own news, never the same theme twice |
| Later | Posted the count and customer quotes, with thanks | more sign-ups from people who already knew him |

Channels: posts to people who already knew him, and DMs. $0 on ads.

**The lesson he learned on counting:** opened, completed and live are three different numbers. A checkout opened is not a payment, and a payment in test mode is not a customer. Say what your number counts.

## Day 0 (1 hour): one buyer, one story, one price
- **One buyer** in one line in `decisions.md`. For example: "founders with a digital product who can't take card payments from where they live".
- **One story that is true for that buyer**, in 3 sentences: what didn't work, what it cost you, that you found a way. The founder tells it in every message, in their own words.
- **One price and a founding offer** with a real cap or a real date, written down. A paid founding spot tells you more than a free trial.
- **Pass:** someone outside your head could read the three lines and know who to message.

## Week 1: people who already know you
- **List 50 names** from your connections, followers and anyone who reacted to your posts. Sort by fit to the one buyer.
- **Post the founding offer** to your own network, in the language that network reads. The author's home-language posts got far more reactions than his English ones.
- **The founder writes to the top 20.** The agent lists them with the angle for each. Link only after "yes".
- **Pass:** 20 written to, every reply answered within a day, every person logged in `customers.md`.

## Week 2: people who showed the problem
- **Mine one post** about the problem with 50+ reactions. Check each reactor; most won't fit.
- **Connection notes, a DM on accept, follow-ups on schedule** (the cadence in `../SKILL.md`, section "Follow-ups").
- **Pass:** 40 people reached in total, every follow-up sent on its day.

## Week 3: ask for the money, in public and in private
- **Everyone who said "that's me"** gets the founding offer, the price and how to start today. Not "would you be interested?".
- **Launch day = post the funnel as it happens:** visitors, sign-ups, sales, in real numbers, with a thank-you.
- **Pass:** every warm "yes, that's me" has received a price. Log each "no" word for word.

## Week 4: count four numbers, fix one
Count in `numbers.md`: **visitors → sign-ups → checkouts started → payments.** Find the biggest drop and fix only that step.
- Remove your own visits and test payments first.
- Replies are not customers. The author's outreach for an earlier product got plenty of replies and made €0. If replies come but nobody pays, look at the offer and the buyer, not the message.
- Fewer than about 20 people at a step: too small to judge. Keep going.
- Write the winning line and the reasons for "no" in `rules.md`.

## Every day (30 min)
1. Replies. 2. Acceptances. 3. Follow-ups due today. 4. Five new people. 5. Log.
Human speed. Stop at any anti-bot check.

## Never at $0
- A redesign before about 50 real visitors a week. Fix distribution first.
- A launch post with nothing to report. Wait for a real number.
- "Free forever" plans to get users. A founding cohort with a cap and a date tells you who buys; free-forever users don't.
- A forecast as the hook. The author's "we launched" posts did far better than his forecast posts.
