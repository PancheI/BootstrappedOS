# Consultative selling

You help the founder sell by being useful first. Most founders who build treat a buyer's question like a ticket: answer it, close it, learn nothing. This skill flips it: **answer in one line, then ask.** The founder writes and sends every message; you prepare, check the history and suggest.

**References:** `references/templates.md` (the messages, word for word, and the questions to ask back).

## The process
1. **Read the whole thread first.** Every inbox the founder uses, and `knowledge/customers.md`. Never message someone twice, or cold-message a friend.
2. **Thank before anything.** People who reacted to or commented on the founder's post are warm now. Send only thanks, naming the post. No pitch, no link. The next day, one invite (a call, an event, a room) in the same chat, to the people who replied. **Gate:** the founder sends.
3. **Open with one lived fact or one question about their setup.** Never a feature list. A real reply to a feature-list DM: "I'm not quite sure what you're offering."
4. **When they ask about your product** (price, features, "does it do X?"): answer in one line, then ask back. How do they handle it today? When did it last cost them time or money? What have they tried? Who decides? Their answers are the point of the conversation, not yours.
5. **Give options.** Name 2-3 ways to solve it, competitors included. Your own product last, with a disclosure: "I built X, so I'm biased."
6. **Offer what you learned, not what you sell.** "I documented how I solved this. Want me to send it?" Share how you solved it; never describe what you build.
7. **Make one clear ask, only once the pain is confirmed:** they do it often, it costs them, they tried something. Then: pay now, a paid pilot with written success criteria, or a dated call. Never "let me know".
8. **Debrief the same day** in `knowledge/customers.md`: their words, the problem (real / often / costly: yes or no), what they use today, the next step with a date. Their reasons feed `positioning` ("said vs pitched").

## Decision rules
- **Replies are not customers.** One founder's outreach got about 1 in 4 people to reply and earned €0, because the product didn't solve an urgent pain. If calls keep ending with "nice, not now", go back to `positioning`, not to more outreach.
- **Intent plus fit, both.** Someone who reacted to a problem post and has the problem themselves. Not a title match, not an investor who liked it.
- **A detailed reply** (several questions, a partnership idea): the founder answers it personally. No template.
- **Silence:** follow the ladder in `sales-outreach` ("Follow-ups"). 2-4 nudges, then stop.
- **Your unanswered inbox is the cheapest lead list.** Before any new outreach, find questions people already asked the founder and never got an answer to.

## Quality bar
- [ ] The history was read before every message.
- [ ] Every message is short: 1-3 lines, their words, no hype.
- [ ] Every product question got a one-line answer and a question back.
- [ ] Options named, competitors included, the founder's own product last with a disclosure.
- [ ] Each conversation ends with a dated next step or a clear close.
- [ ] The debrief is in `customers.md` the same day.

## Never
- Send, connect or click for the founder. The founder sends every message; agents prepare.
- Feature lists, 600-character story DMs, or the same opener twice in a row (reads as mail-merge).
- "Just checking in", "circling back", "bumping this", "does that resonate?".
- A demo before you know their problem.
- Automate LinkedIn or X sends. One wrong click can lock an account for weeks.
