# Grants & credits

**This week:** credits and perks you qualify for, checked on the official page, form answers drafted. **Over time:** grants that fit, drafted from your answer bank. The agent finds, checks and drafts; the founder applies.

**References:** none. Programmes open now come from the core's `funding`; your answer bank is `knowledge/how-to/grants-answers.md` (budget format in "Drafting a grant" below).

**Be honest with the founder:** nothing is guaranteed; grants take weeks to months and most need a registered company. With no company yet, stick to credits and perks.

## What the author has actually secured
| What | How it came | What it taught |
|---|---|---|
| **300,000 OP**, a public ecosystem grants program, Phase 1 (paid Nov 2022) | A public forum proposal, reviewed by delegates, voted on | The first version was pushed back; the resubmitted budget won. Budget lines must create usage, not reach |
| **An AI-voice company's startup grant** (30M credits, per the author) | Their startup grants form | Once secured, show *their* official badge ("Supported by ... Grants"), never a homemade logo |
| **A free year of an AI lab's team plan + $1,000 API credits** | Claimed the week it opened | Paused a few days later after hundreds of thousands of applications. Speed is the whole game for credits |
| **Up to $45k in partner AI-tool offers** (the program's published figure) | Same program, partner list | Still open after the headline offer paused; the long tail outlives the headline |

## The 4 kinds, fastest first
1. **AI and cloud credits.** A form and a few days. They cut the bills you pay today. Many close within 2-3 days of going public: check the day you hear.
2. **Tool perks.** Free months or discounts on tools you use or will use within 3 months. A free tool you don't need is a future bill.
3. **Grants and public funding.** The biggest amounts, the longest forms, weeks to months. Start one only when the fit is strong and the budget can be tied to usage.
4. **Accelerators and competitions without equity.** Read the terms: anything that takes equity or a revenue share says so on the first line.

## Qualify first
Read CLAUDE.md for: country and where the company is registered, entity type (or none yet), company age, stage, revenue band, team size, industry, tools in use, funding raised. Ask for anything missing once, in one list. A program that doesn't fit is noise; drop it.

## The process (weekly, live, never from memory)
1. **Qualify** (section above). Ask for anything missing once, in one list.
2. Search the web for AI and cloud credits, startup programmes, tool perks and grants, filtered by country, industry and tools in use.
3. Open the **official page** for every candidate and read today's terms: who qualifies, what you get, for how long, the deadline, what you give back.
4. Cite the link and the date checked. Can't reach the official page? "unverified", not ranked.
5. Never state an amount, a deadline or eligibility from memory or from a blog list.
6. Update the tracker (below). Rank by value x fit x speed. Pick one to draft.
7. **Draft** a credit form or a grant ("Drafting a grant" below). **Gate:** the founder reads, decides and presses submit.

## Drafting a grant (the author's structure)
1. **Copy the program's own fields, in their order, with their word limits.** His proposal answered 26 fields exactly as the program listed them, from "Number of tokens requested" to "Why will users stay after incentives end".
2. **Lead with one hard number and its date.** His: the dollar value of orders placed on the product before the program's snapshot date. Small and exact beats big and vague.
3. **Budget: every line = share + amount + what triggers the spend + how long.** Not "marketing 50%". His lines read like "20% - gas refunds, paid continuously as users swap" and "20% - an airdrop, released after the first 1,360 limit orders are filled".
4. **Fund usage, not reach.** His first version put 50% into influencer marketing. A delegate recommended a no ("difficult to justify"). The resubmission moved it into a liquidity pool, gas refunds and an airdrop tied to real orders; the review committee recommended yes ("a clear and well-thought-out plan for distribution").
5. **Answer "what happens after the money" before they ask.** Every program cares whether users stay when the incentive ends.
6. **Public forum program? Reply in the thread and revise.** The comments are the review. A revised proposal is normal, not a failure.
7. Every number from CLAUDE.md, the numbers skill or the founder. Missing: `[NEED: ...]`. Never invent traction, users, revenue or letters of support.
8. End with the founder's checklist: what to review, attach, where to submit, by when.

## Talking about it (in public and in your own offer)
- **Never "free money".** The author: "it looks scammy". Say "grants and AI credits, no equity" in headlines and "non-dilutive funding" in labels. "Free AI credits" as a plain fact is fine.
- **Never publish perk codes or per-partner amounts.** The codes are the benefit. Express the value in a unit the reader decides in ("worth more than your first four months") and recompute it when partners change.
- **Unsecured = "on the way".** Never promise a perk before it's signed.
- **Check public words against the code list.** He nearly used a word as a public comment trigger that was also a partner's discount code.

## Decision rules
- **Buy your domain and a company email first.** A free subdomain counts as no domain, and many credit forms refuse a free email address.
- **Sign up for a partner offer with the same email as your programme account.** The author found one partner's offer only applied when the emails matched.
- **Claim a "never paid" offer before the first invoice.** Offers for new customers stop applying once you've paid that tool.
- **Codes you receive are yours to use, not yours to share.** Passing them on can cost you the perk.
- **Speed wins for credits.** Check the day you hear; many close within days.
- **No company yet:** credits and perks only. Grants come later.
- **Grant budget:** every line tied to usage, never to reach (the author's resubmission won on exactly that).

## Quality bar
- [ ] Every row in the tracker has the official link and the date checked; unreachable pages say "unverified" and aren't ranked.
- [ ] No amount, deadline or eligibility from memory or a blog list.
- [ ] Each draft follows the programme's own fields, order and word limits; every number has a source or `[NEED: ...]`.
- [ ] The budget has share + amount + trigger + duration on every line.
- [ ] The founder's checklist ends each draft: what to review, attach, where, by when.

## Never
- Submit an application, accept terms, or create an account in the founder's name.
- Enter payment details, ID documents, tax IDs or bank details anywhere.
- Claim a credit or grant is secured before the founder confirms it.
- Recommend a program that takes equity without saying so on the first line.
