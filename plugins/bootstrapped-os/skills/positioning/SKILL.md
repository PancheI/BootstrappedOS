# Positioning

A wedge can be real and still be positioned wrong. A product can answer real, linked complaints and still sell something a generic AI chat gives away free, for example a set of reminder emails for a monthly fee. A beautiful page around that offer still makes no sense. This step asks "why would anyone pay us, instead of what they already do?" before anything is built.

This step answers that, in writing, before the brand, the price or the page exist. Its output is one positioning card that every later stage reads. The founder picks the promise; the agent drafts the card and the roast.

**References:** `references/alternatives.md` (what buyers use today, and how to rank it) · `references/card.md` (the positioning card).

## The process
1. **Read the evidence, not the idea.** `knowledge/ideas/` (signals, the chosen wedge), `knowledge/founder.md`. Work only from what buyers said; mark anything you assume.
2. **List what they do today, ranked by how many signals mention it** (`references/alternatives.md`). Always include three: **doing nothing** (wait, absorb the loss), **a generic AI chat** (ChatGPT, Claude: free, instant), and **the spreadsheet or email they already use**. Then the paid tools with real prices, read on their pages today.
3. **Said vs pitched.** Count the reasons buyers give in their own words. When the author counted his own buyers, few gave a reason in their own words, and the reason he pitched almost never came up. Position on the most-said reason, not the one you like.
4. **Find what only you can do** against each alternative. Not "faster" or "easier". A thing the alternative cannot do at all: knows the rule, has the data, takes the action, carries the weight. If a generic AI chat can do it from one prompt, it is not your position.
5. **Name the outcome in money or time**, in the buyer's words, with one number: "€40 plus interest on every late invoice", "paid this month, not in 90 days", "a price before anyone has paid". Never a feature count ("five emails").
6. **The roast (from the author's audits).** Answer, in one line each:
   - Why would anyone pay this price instead of the top alternative?
   - What do they get back, in their money? (ROI from their own numbers: "one recovered invoice pays for a year".)
   - What is the one objection a buyer says first, and its answer?
   Any line you can't answer with evidence: the wedge goes back to research, or the offer changes. Don't paper over it with copy.
7. **One promise, few parts** (the author's rule after a week of stacking: "we are making the offer complex instead of streamlined"). One sentence the buyer would repeat to a friend. Everything else is proof underneath.
8. **Write the positioning card** (`references/card.md`) and show it to the founder with the roast. **Gate:** wait for the founder's yes before brand, pricing or page.

## Decision rules
- **A generic AI chat does it for free:** your position is what the chat can't: the rule it doesn't know, the data you hold, the action you take for them, the follow-through over days. If there's nothing, kill or reshape the wedge.
- **The buyer's pain is the other party's behaviour** (late payers, slow agencies, silent clients): position on leverage, not on better wording. People pay for what moves the other side.
- **Money is on the table that buyers don't claim** (statutory interest, fees they can recover, credits they qualify for): lead with it, after you've verified the rule on an official source for the buyer's country, dated.
- **Two reasons tie:** pick the one with money attached.
- **Price comes after this card,** never before. The roast's ROI line sets the ceiling the CFO prices under.

## Quality bar
- [ ] At least three alternatives, including doing nothing and a generic AI chat, each with what it costs today (time or money).
- [ ] The outcome has one number and is in the buyer's words.
- [ ] "Only we" survives the test: could a generic AI chat do it from one prompt? No.
- [ ] Every roast line answered with evidence, or the wedge sent back.
- [ ] One sentence a buyer would repeat. No feature count in it.
- [ ] Every claim of law, money or data has a source and a date.

## Never
- Position on a feature list or a count of things ("5 emails", "9 hires", "all-in-one").
- Pick the reason you'd like to sell over the one buyers said.
- Claim a legal right or a sum without the official source for that country.
- Fix a positioning problem with design or copy. A better page around a weak offer is still a weak offer.
